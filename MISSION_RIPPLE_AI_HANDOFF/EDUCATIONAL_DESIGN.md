# Educational design

Target: children aged 7–12. The intended thinking loop is **SEE → UNDERSTAND → THINK → DECIDE → OBSERVE → EXPLAIN**. The implemented card UI abbreviates it visually to **See → Understand → Choose → Result**. Children first see a rover icon and small bar sequence, then a day comparison, then two option cards with now/later effects, then a short debrief. Nuri gives one idea at a time. The exact ten selected values, source URL, IDs and hash sit in an optional collapsed source drawer rather than a default wall of text.

Writing rule: short sentences, familiar terms, one idea per block, large readable contrast. Explain any technical term immediately. Identify the instrument and location before interpreting a number. Do not use unsupported green-safe/red-danger labels for this radiation case. Distinguish **real NASA rover data** from a **training-game choice** with visible tags. Uncertainty is a lesson: children can learn to say that the cause and human dose are not known. Both scheduling options have trade-offs, not a simple moralized right answer.

Current assessment is implicit in completing exploration and branch/debrief, with no formal mastery measurement or child usability test. Accessibility present: bilingual English/Arabic and RTL DOM direction, keyboard input, readable cards and responsive layout; gaps include screen-reader audit, contrast audit, accessible chart alternatives and child language testing. Future improvements should be validated with children and preserve authentic measurement context. Main files: `src/science/ui.ts`, `src/style.css`, `src/main.ts`; QA `tools/science-qa.mjs`.
